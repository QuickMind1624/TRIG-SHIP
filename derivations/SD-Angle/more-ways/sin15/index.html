01 · What do we already know?

Before calculating anything, collect the facts that are already known. We will use these known trigonometric values to reach the smaller 15° angle.

Known values

The 30° and 45° angles give us four values we can use immediately.

sin(30°)=12
sin(45°)=1√2
cos(30°)=√32
cos(45°)=1√2

Our target is the smaller angle between the 30° and 45° directions. So the quantity we ultimately want is sin(15°).

We will find sin(15°) with the help of sin(30°) and sin(45°).
The basic language of the triangle

What do sine and cosine actually mean here?

For a right triangle, choose an angle x. Relative to that angle, the side directly opposite it is the perpendicular, the side next to it is the base, and the longest side opposite the right angle is the hypotenuse.

sin(x)=perpendicularhypotenuse
cos(x)=basehypotenuse

This is why the lengths in our construction matter: once the hypotenuse is known, a sine or cosine value can tell us the corresponding side length.

02 · What does the construction give us?

Now we stop looking at the diagram as one large picture. We pick out one fact at a time and let each fact lead to the next one.

First clue · equal unit lengths

BE = BA = 1 unit

The construction gives us two equal lengths from the same starting point B:

BE=BA=1  unit

Therefore, in △BCE, the side BE is the hypotenuse, and in △BAD, the side BA is the hypotenuse. These two hypotenuses have the same length: 1 unit.

That repeated unit length is the important clue to keep in mind. A unit length can also be viewed as a radius, so the construction naturally connects the two right triangles with the idea of a unit circle.

Second clue · find AD

Use the 45° triangle first.

In △BAD, the angle at B is 45°, the hypotenuse is AB, and the side opposite 45° is AD. Therefore:

ADAB=sin(45°)
ADAB=1√2

We already know from the construction that AB = 1 unit. Substitute that value:

AD1=1√2→AD = 1√2

So the vertical segment AD is now known. We have not guessed its length; it came directly from the 45° sine ratio and the unit hypotenuse.

Third clue · find BD

The same 45° triangle also gives us BD.

In the same right triangle, BD is the side adjacent to the 45° angle. Therefore cosine gives:

BDAB=cos(45°)
BDAB=1√2

Since AB = 1 unit:

BD=1√2

Now we know BD. That matters because BD is the side that the 30° triangle will use next.

Fourth clue · enter the 30° triangle

BD is common to the next triangle.

Look at △BOD. The angle at B is 30°, and the right angle is at D. Therefore BO is the hypotenuse and BD is the side adjacent to 30°.

BDOB=cos(30°)
cos(30°)=sin(60°)=√32
BDOB=√32

We already found BD = 1/√2. To isolate OB, divide BD by the cosine value:

OB=BDcos(30°)=1/√2√3/2

Dividing by a fraction means multiplying by its reciprocal:

OB=1√2×2√3=√63
Fifth clue · find OD

Now sine gives the vertical part of △BOD.

For the 30° angle in △BOD, OD is opposite the angle and OB is the hypotenuse. So:

ODOB=sin(30°)
ODOB=12

We have already found OB = √6/3. Substitute it:

OD=OB × 12=√63×12=√66

So OD = √6/6, which is the same as 1/√6. Both forms represent the same length.

Alternative route: use tan(30°)

In △BOD, relative to 30°, OD is opposite and BD is adjacent:

tan(30°)=ODBD=1√3
OD=BD × 1√3=1√2×1√3=1√6

Alternative route: use Pythagoras

△BOD is right-angled at D, so the hypotenuse is OB. Therefore:

OB²=BD² + OD²
OD²=OB² − BD²
OD²=(√63)2−(1√2)2
OD²=(√6)²3²−1²(√2)²
OD²=69−12=23−12=16
OD=√16=1√6

03 · Connect the two constructions

We now use the lengths we have just uncovered to move toward the inner triangle. Nothing new is guessed here; we simply read the large triangle as a sum of two smaller vertical pieces.

First connection

AD is made from AO and OD.

Point O lies on the vertical segment AD. Therefore the whole segment AD is exactly the sum of the two parts AO and OD:

AD=AO+OD

We already know both quantities on the right: AD = 1/√2 and OD = 1/√6. So AO is obtained by subtracting the lower piece OD from the whole length AD:

AO=AD − OD=1√2−1√6

This gives us the length AO, which belongs to the smaller triangle △AOM. That is the triangle we will use next.

Angle clue · why 75°?

The angle ∠OMB becomes 75° from the perpendicular construction.

At B, the upper ray BA is 45° above the horizontal, while the lower ray BO is 30° above the same horizontal. Therefore the angle between BA and BO is:

∠MBO=45° − 30°=15°

The segment MO is drawn perpendicular to the 30° ray BO. A perpendicular creates a 90° angle. So in the right triangle △BMO, the remaining two angles must add up to 90°:

∠OMB=90° − 15°=75°

That is why the 75° mark is there. It is not an independent fact: it follows from the 15° angle at B and the right angle made by MO ⟂ BO.

04 · Build the final 15° triangle

Now we use the lengths we have already found to construct the triangle whose angle is 15°. The goal is not to jump to the answer; every new length will come from something we have already established.

Auxiliary construction for the next length calculations.

Deep look · where does 105° come from?

There are two useful ways to see the same angle. First, the 30° ray is perpendicular to the line that reaches M, so the corresponding angle made with the horizontal is 60°. At M, the other ray is the 45° side. Therefore the large angle is simply the two visible pieces together:

105°=60°+45°

There is also a triangle-angle check. In △OAM, the angle at O is 30° and the angle at A is 45°. Since the three interior angles of a triangle add to 180°:

∠OMA=180°−30°−45°=105°

So the 105° mark is not an assumed value. Both pieces of geometry point to the same angle.

First clue · split AO

Turn the known length AO into two smaller horizontal pieces.

We already calculated the complete length AO. Now divide that same segment into two parts, called x and y. Because the two pieces together make the whole segment:

x+y=AO

From the previous box:

AO=1√2−1√6=√3−1√6
Second clue · express x and y using h

The same height h is shared by both right triangles.

For the left small right triangle, the angle at the base is 30°. Therefore:

tan(30°)=hx=1√3

To make x the subject, multiply both sides by x and then by √3:

h=x√3→x=h√3

For the right small triangle, the base angle is 45°:

tan(45°)=hy=1
h=y→y=h

So the two pieces are now written using the single unknown height h:

x+y=h√3+h=h(√3+1)
Third clue · solve for h

Both expressions describe the same segment AO.

We have two descriptions of x+y. One comes from the geometry of the two smaller triangles, and the other comes from the length AO that we already calculated. Therefore we can equate them:

h(√3+1)=√3−1√6

Now divide both sides by √3+1:

h=√3−1√3+1×1√6

At this point the fraction containing √3+1 is not in a convenient form. So we rationalize it by multiplying the numerator and denominator by its conjugate √3−1:

h=√3−1√3+1×√3−1√3−1×1√6
=(√3−1)²(√3)²−1²×1√6
=3−2√3+12×1√6=2−√3√6
h=2−√3√6

Why multiply by √3−1?

The denominator contains √3+1. Multiplying by its conjugate turns the denominator into a difference of squares:

(√3+1)(√3−1)=3−1=2

So the radical disappears from that denominator, making the expression much easier to simplify.

Fourth clue · find AM

Use the 45° triangle to turn h into the side AM.

In the right triangle containing AM, the angle at A is 45°. The side opposite that angle is h, while AM=t is the hypotenuse. Therefore:

sin(45°)=ht=1√2

Rearrange to make t the subject:

t=h√2=2−√3√3

So AM is now known. We will use it to find the missing hypotenuse of the 15° triangle.

Fifth clue · find MB

The original unit length gives us the missing hypotenuse.

We already considered AB = 1 unit. Point M lies between A and B, so the complete side AB is split into AM and MB:

MB+AM=AB=1

Therefore:

MB=1−AM=1−2−√3√3=2(√3−1)√3

This is the key reason we needed AM: the 15° triangle is already visible, but its hypotenuse is MB, not the full unit segment AB. Subtracting AM from the known unit length gives exactly that missing hypotenuse.

Sixth clue · find OM

The same 30° relationship gives us OM.

In the right triangle containing OM=z and h, the angle is 30°. Here h is opposite and z is the hypotenuse:

sin(30°)=hz=12

Multiply both sides by 2z and divide by 1:

z=2h=2(2−√3)√6

So OM=z is now completely known.

Final clue · the 15° triangle is ready

Now the target sine is simply opposite ÷ hypotenuse.

The small right triangle at B has angle 15°. Its opposite side is OM=z, and its hypotenuse is MB. Therefore:

sin(15°)=zMB

Substitute the two lengths we have just found:

sin(15°)=2(2−√3)/√62(√3−1)/√3

Rewrite the complex fraction as multiplication by the reciprocal:

=2(2−√3)√6×√32(√3−1)

Cancel the common factor 2 and simplify:

=(2−√3)√3√6(√3−1)=√2(2−√3)2(√3−1)

Rationalize the remaining denominator using the conjugate √3+1:

=√2(2−√3)(√3+1)2(3−1)=√2(√3−1)4
sin(15°)=√6−√24
One last mathematical note

Could the Law of Sines be used here?

In principle, the Law of Sines can be written for the relevant triangle. But it would require a value such as sin(105°) to obtain the needed side directly. In this derivation, introducing that value through the same angle relationships we are trying to establish would make the argument circular. So we deliberately stay with the right-triangle relations already available to us.

asin A=bsin B=csin C

Alternative route · Law of Sines

Yes — the Law of Sines can be used here. This route is kept as an alternative because it takes a different path through the same geometry. We first find sin(105°), then use the Law of Sines in △OAM to find its remaining sides.

Step 1 · Find sin(105°)

We already know the two angles 60° and 45°, so:

105°=60° + 45°

Using the angle-sum identity:

sin(105°)=sin(60°+45°)
=sin60° cos45°+cos60° sin45°
=√32×1√2+12×1√2
=√3+12√2=√6+√24
sin(105°)=√6+√24
Step 2 · Connect 105° with 15°

Now notice that:

105°=90° + 15°

Apply the sine angle-sum identity again:

sin(105°)=sin(90°+15°)
=sin90° cos15°+cos90° sin15°

Because sin90° = 1 and cos90° = 0, the second term disappears:

sin(105°)=cos15°

So the value we have already found for sin(105°) is also the value of cos(15°):

cos(15°)=√6+√24
Step 3 · Recover sin(15°)

For the same angle, sine and cosine satisfy:

sin²(15°)+cos²(15°)=1
sin²(15°)=1−(√6+√24)2
=1−8+4√316
=8−4√316=2−√34

Since 15° is an acute angle, its sine is positive:

sin(15°)=√2−√34=√6−√24
sin(15°)=√6−√24
Step 4 · Now use the Law of Sines in △OAM

Now the important part: sin(105°) is known, so the Law of Sines can be used directly.

In △OAM, side OA is opposite 105°, OM is opposite 45°, and AM is opposite 30°:

OAsin105°=OMsin45°=AMsin30°

We already know:

OA=1√2−1√6
sin105°=√6+√24
sin45°=1√2
sin30°=12

For OM:

OM=OA × sin45°sin105°
=√3−1√6×1/√2(√6+√2)/4
=2√63−√2
OM=2√63−√2

For AM:

AM=OA × sin30°sin105°
=√3−1√6×1/2(√6+√2)/4
AM=2√33−1

So the Law of Sines gives the remaining two sides of △OAM:

OM=2√63−√2
AM=2√33−1

Important: this is an alternative route, not a replacement for the main geometric derivation. Once the angle-sum identity is available, this route is completely valid. It simply uses more previously established trigonometric machinery before applying the Law of Sines.

BD=1  unit

02 · Use the two connected right triangles

The first triangle gives us BC and CD. The second triangle uses the same BC to give us BA and AC. That common side is the bridge between the two triangles.

Triangle BCD · find BC

BD is the hypotenuse, and ∠CBD = 30°.

Since C is the right angle, BD is the hypotenuse. Relative to 30°, BC is the adjacent side:

BCBD=cos(30°)
BC1=√32
BC=√32

We can also find the vertical part CD from sine:

CDBD=sin(30°)
CD=12
Triangle BCA · use the common BC

Now the same BC belongs to the 45° right triangle.

Because C is a right angle and the angle at B is 45°, triangle BCA is a 45°–45°–90° triangle. Here BC is adjacent to 45° and BA is the hypotenuse:

BCBA=cos(45°)
√3/2BA=1√2

Multiply both sides by BA and solve for the hypotenuse:

BA=BC × √2
=√32×√2
BA=√62

Since triangle BCA is 45°–45°–90°, its two legs are equal:

AC=BC=√32
Bridge to triangle NDA

Find AD by subtracting the two vertical pieces.

Point D lies between C and A, so the full vertical length AC is split into CD and DA:

AD=AC − CD
=√32−12
AD=√3−12
AD=√3−12

03 · The dual-triangle split

Now we focus only on triangle NDA. The altitude from N to AD creates two right triangles. This is the key DTM step: one side is shared as the same height h, while the two base angles are 30° and 45°.

The original DTM split: triangle NDA is divided into two right triangles by NT.

What the labels mean

DT = x, TA = y, NT = h, ND = z, and NA = m.

The two smaller triangles share the same perpendicular height h.

Angles of △NDA

Why are the angles 30°, 45°, and 105°?

At D, the 30° angle is already part of the construction. At A, the 45° angle comes from the original 45° triangle. Therefore the remaining angle at N is:

∠DNA=180° − 30° − 45°
∠DNA=105°
△NDA has angles 30°, 45°, and 105°.
Left right triangle · △DNT

Use the 30° triangle to connect x, h, and z.

In △DNT, the angle at D is 30°, NT = h is opposite 30°, DT = x is adjacent, and ND = z is the hypotenuse.

hx=tan(30°)=1√3
h=x√3→x=h√3

Now use sine for the same triangle:

hz=sin(30°)=12
z=2h
Right right triangle · △NTA

Use the 45° triangle to connect y, h, and m.

In △NTA, the angle at A is 45°. The opposite side is NT = h, the adjacent side is TA = y, and the hypotenuse is NA = m.

hy=tan(45°)=1
y=h

And from sine 45°:

hm=sin(45°)=1√2
m=h√2
The shared base · solve h

Both small triangles together make the known side AD.

Because DT = x and TA = y:

AD=x + y
=h√3 + h
=h(√3+1)

But from the previous box we already know:

AD=√3−12

Equate the two expressions for the same side AD:

h(√3+1)=√3−12
h=√3−12(√3+1)

Rationalize the denominator:

h=√3−12(√3+1)×√3−1√3−1
=(√3−1)²2(3−1)
=4−2√34
h=2−√32
h=2−√32

04 · Finish the 15° triangle

The DTM has now reduced the problem to two ordinary right triangles. We can recover the needed sides, then use triangle BDN to read off sin(15°).

Recover ND and NA

The common height h gives both sloping sides.

ND=z=2h
z=2×2−√32
ND=2−√3
NA=m=h√2
m=2−√32×√2
NA=√2 − √62
ND=2−√3
NA=√2−√62
Find BN

N lies on BA, so subtract NA from BA.

Because N is on the straight segment BA:

BA=BN + NA
BN=BA − NA
=√62−(√2 − √62)
BN=√6−√2
BN=√6−√2
Target · sin(15°)

Now return to the small right triangle BDN.

At B, the angle between BD and BN is the required 15°. Since ∠BDN = 90°, BN is the hypotenuse and DN is the opposite side.

sin(15°)=DNBN
=2−√3√6−√2

Rationalize the denominator:

sin(15°)=(2−√3)(√6+√2)(√6−√2)(√6+√2)
=(2−√3)(√6+√2)4
=√6−√24
sin(15°)=√6−√24

Alternative route · Law of Sines

This route is kept separate from the main DTM proof. It uses the side DN already obtained above and then applies the Law of Sines in △BDN as a cross-check route.

Step 1 · Find sin(75°)

At N, the remaining angle in △BDN is:

∠BND=180°−90°−15°=75°

Using the known 30° and 45° values:

75°=45°+30°
sin(75°)=sin(45°+30°)
=sin45° cos30°+cos45° sin30°
=1√2×√32+1√2×12
sin(75°)=√6+√24
Step 2 · Use the Law of Sines in △BDN

Here BD=1, ∠BDN=90°, and ∠BND=75°. Therefore:

BDsin75°=BNsin90°
BN=1sin75°
=4√6+√2
BN=√6−√2
BN=√6−√2
Step 3 · Recover sin(15°)

From the main DTM route, the opposite side is already known:

DN=2−√3

Now the definition of sine in the right triangle BDN gives:

sin(15°)=DNBN
=2−√3√6−√2
=√6−√24
sin(15°)=√6−√24

Note: this is an alternative/check route. It still uses the already-established DTM length DN; it is not intended to replace the main dual-triangle derivation.
