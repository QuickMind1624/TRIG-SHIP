Unit Circle Method

Explore how rotating a point on a circle connects angle addition with sine and cosine. The circle radius stays fixed at 1.

Move the angles

On a unit circle, every point has coordinates (cos θ, sin θ). The horizontal coordinate gives cosine; the vertical coordinate gives sine.

Try changing A and B. Notice how the point for A+B moves around the same circle.

Derivation: why angle addition works

We use a circle of radius 1 centred at the origin O=(0,0). A point at angle θ has coordinates (cos θ, sin θ).

1. Point at angle A
P=(cos A, sin A).
2. Rotate by an additional angle B
The new angle measured from the positive x-axis is A+B. Call the new point Q=(cos(A+B), sin(A+B)).
3. Apply the rotation-coordinate rule
Rotating a point (x,y) anticlockwise by B gives (x cos B − y sin B, x sin B + y cos B).
4. Substitute the coordinates of P
Put x=cos A and y=sin A into the rotation rule.
cos(A+B)=cos A cos B − sin A sin B
sin(A+B)=sin A cos B + cos A sin B

Why does the rotation rule look like this? The new horizontal coordinate combines the old horizontal part x cos B with the horizontal contribution −y sin B; the new vertical coordinate combines x sin B and y cos B. These are the standard coordinate equations for rotating a vector in the Cartesian plane.

This is the compact first-pass derivation. The visualization above lets you test the geometry for different angles.
