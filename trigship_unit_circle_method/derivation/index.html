Unit Circle Method — Derivation

A careful coordinate-geometry derivation of the angle-sum identities.

← Lesson homeOpen visualization →

1. Why use the unit circle?

A circle of radius 1 centred at the origin has equation x² + y² = 1. A point at angle θ, measured anticlockwise from the positive x-axis, has coordinates Pθ = (cos θ, sin θ). Normally cos θ = adjacent/hypotenuse and sin θ = opposite/hypotenuse. Here the hypotenuse is the radius, 1, so the coordinates directly become cos θ and sin θ.

This is not a new definition disconnected from triangles: it is the right-triangle definition expressed as coordinates, extended around the whole circle.

2. Put two angles on the circle

Let the first angle be A and the second angle be B. On the unit circle define

P = (cos A, sin A)
Q = (cos(A+B), sin(A+B))

The angle between the radii OP and OQ is B, because (A+B) − A = B. The chord PQ therefore has a length determined by B alone. We will calculate that same length in two different ways.

3. Find PQ using the distance formula

For points (x₁,y₁) and (x₂,y₂), horizontal separation is x₂−x₁ and vertical separation is y₂−y₁. Pythagoras gives the distance formula:

PQ² = (x₂−x₁)² + (y₂−y₁)²

Substitute the coordinates of P and Q:

PQ² = [cos(A+B) − cos A]² + [sin(A+B) − sin A]²

Expand both squares, keeping each term:

= cos²(A+B) − 2cos(A+B)cos A + cos²A
+ sin²(A+B) − 2sin(A+B)sin A + sin²A

Group the square terms. The unit-circle identity cos²θ + sin²θ = 1 applies to both angles:

PQ² = 1 − 2cos(A+B)cos A + 1 − 2sin(A+B)sin A
PQ² = 2 − 2[cos(A+B)cos A + sin(A+B)sin A] (1)

4. Find the same chord using triangle geometry

Triangle OPQ has OP = OQ = 1 and included angle POQ = B. By the cosine rule:

PQ² = OP² + OQ² − 2(OP)(OQ)cos B

Because OP = OQ = 1, their squares are 1 and their product is 1:

PQ² = 1 + 1 − 2cos B = 2 − 2cos B (2)

5. Equate the two expressions

Both (1) and (2) equal the same quantity PQ². Therefore their right-hand sides are equal:

2 − 2[cos(A+B)cos A + sin(A+B)sin A] = 2 − 2cos B

Subtract 2 from both sides, then divide both sides by −2:

cos(A+B)cos A + sin(A+B)sin A = cos B

Write X = A+B and Y = A. Then X−Y = B, so this equation is cos(X−Y) = cos X cos Y + sin X sin Y. This is the cosine difference identity. Replace Y with −B and X with A; using cos(−B)=cos B and sin(−B)=−sin B gives the cosine sum identity. The sine sum identity follows by applying the cosine sum identity to the complementary angle 90°−(A+B).

cos(A+B) = cos A cos B − sin A sin B
sin(A+B) = sin A cos B + cos A sin B

Key idea: the distance formula and cosine rule describe the same chord. Matching them forces the angle-sum identity.

6. Difference identities

Replace B with −B in the sum identities. Since cos(−B)=cos B and sin(−B)=−sin B:

sin(A−B) = sin A cos B − cos A sin B
cos(A−B) = cos A cos B + sin A sin B

These signs are not memorised at random: they come from the even/odd properties of cosine and sine.
