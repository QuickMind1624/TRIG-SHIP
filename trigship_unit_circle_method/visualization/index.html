Quick doubt · Why radius = 1?

For a point at angle θ on a circle of radius r, its coordinates are (r cos θ, r sin θ). If r=1, the coordinates become (cos θ, sin θ). The right-triangle hypotenuse is 1, so cos θ = adjacent/1 and sin θ = opposite/1. That removes an extra multiplier and lets us use cosine and sine directly as the x- and y-coordinates.

Quick doubt · Why is the angle −B?

Angles measured anticlockwise are positive by convention; clockwise turns are negative. R is B clockwise from the positive x-axis, so its angle is −B. The directed angular difference is A−(−B)=A+B. The same geometry works for every angle setting: compare the smaller angle between the two rays, not necessarily the full turn shown by the angle controls.

Quick doubt · Why do we compare OPR and OQS?

OP=OQ=1 and OR=OS=1 because all four are radii. The rays are separated by the same angle in both triangles: from OP to OR the directed change is A−(−B)=A+B, and from OQ to OS it is (A+B)−0=A+B. A triangle’s interior angle is the smaller angle between its two sides, so both included angles match even when the displayed turn passes 180°. SAS gives congruent triangles and PR = QS.

How the points are constructed

P = (cos A, sin A)

P sits on the circle at angle A. Because the radius is 1, its horizontal coordinate is cos A and vertical coordinate is sin A.

Q = (cos(A+B), sin(A+B))

From OP, turn anticlockwise through another B. The total angle from the x-axis is A+B, so Q receives that angle in its coordinate definition.

R = (cos(−B), sin(−B))

Turn clockwise by B from the x-axis. Using even/odd properties, this is also R = (cos B, −sin B).

S = (1,0)

S is where the positive x-axis meets the unit circle: one unit right of O, with zero vertical displacement.

The two triangles that matter

The highlighted triangles are △OPR and △OQS. They are not selected because their shaded areas are equal; the important facts are their two equal radii and equal included angles.

OP = OQ = 1;   OR = OS = 1;   ∠POR and ∠QOS have the same smaller angle

By SAS congruence, △OPR ≅ △OQS. Corresponding sides are equal, so PR = QS. The next page uses the coordinates of P, R, Q and S to calculate these two lengths.
