Pythagoras → Trigonometric Identities

See exactly how the right-triangle theorem becomes the fundamental identity, then use that identity to derive the other two Pythagorean identities.

Right triangle: the starting point

Hypotenuse (H) Perpendicular (P) Base (B)

Route A — Pythagoras → sin²θ + cos²θ = 1

P² + B² = H²
  1. Divide every term by H².
  2. P²H² + B²H² = H²H²
  3. (PH)² + (BH)² = 1
  4. Use sin θ = PH and cos θ = BH.
  5. sin²θ + cos²θ = 1

Route B — Unit circle

For a unit circle, the radius is 1. The point at angle θ has coordinates (cos θ, sin θ).

x² + y² = 1
  1. Put x = cos θ.
  2. Put y = sin θ.
  3. Substitute into the unit-circle equation.
  4. cos²θ + sin²θ = 1

Then derive the other identities

Divide by cos²θ:

sin²θcos²θ + 1 = 1cos²θ

tan²θ + 1 = sec²θ

Divide by sin²θ:

1 + cos²θsin²θ = 1sin²θ

1 + cot²θ = cosec²θ

The whole chain

P² + B² = H² ÷ H² (PH)² + (BH)² = 1 definitions sin²θ + cos²θ = 1 ÷ cos²θ 1 + tan²θ = sec²θ ÷ sin²θ 1 + cot²θ = cosec²θ
